Labelled data for lexical complexity prediction exist for few languages. We predict the complexity of words in a language using only the labelled data of other languages. Our method combines two signals. The first is a ridge regression and gradient-boosted trees, trained on word features and on the embeddings of three frozen multilingual encoders. The second is the expected rating of Gemma~4 12B, an open-weight language model used zero-shot without fine-tuning. A weighted average fuses the two signals. No label of the test language is used for training or for choosing settings. We evaluate with a leave-one-language-out protocol on the ten languages of MLSP 2024 and on Romanian. Over these eleven languages, the mean Pearson correlation is 0.693, with a 95\% interval of 0.679 to 0.706. On the ten MLSP 2024 languages, the mean is 0.690. The highest published mean on MLSP 2024 is 0.669, from a method that reads 30 labels of each evaluated language.
