We predicted lexical complexity in a language without using any of its labels. Our method combines two signals. Signal~A is a ridge regression and HGB, trained on the embeddings of three frozen multilingual encoders and on four word features. Signal~B is the expected rating of Gemma~4 over three zero-shot prompts. A weighted average fuses the two signals. We chose the language model, the temperature, the grouping and the weights on the other languages. Over eleven languages, the mean Pearson correlation is 0.693, with a 95\% interval of 0.679 to 0.706. The fusion is above both signals in ten of the eleven languages. The correlation is lowest on Sinhala (0.430) and Filipino (0.504). On the ten MLSP 2024 languages, the mean is 0.690. The highest published mean on these languages is 0.669, from a method that reads 30 labels of each evaluated language. The eye-tracking data did not help the multilingual method. They exist only for Romanian texts. In an earlier version of the method, they did not bring a significant gain in correlation on Romanian. The increase was 0.007, with a 95\% interval of $-0.001$ to 0.014, which includes zero. Claude Opus 5.5, used zero-shot, reaches 0.721 over the eleven languages, which is above our method.
