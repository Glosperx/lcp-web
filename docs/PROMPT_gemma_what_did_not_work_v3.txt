We conducted these experiments on earlier versions of the method using the ten MLSP languages unless otherwise specified. Each difference is reported relative to the version of the method used at that time.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Eye-tracking dataset.} For Romanian, we included eye-tracking measures from 95 readers in MultiplEYE \citep{jakobi-etal-2025-multipleye}. These raised the correlation by 0.007, with a 95\% interval of $-0.001$ to 0.014, which includes zero. This is an upper bound because the weight of the measures was chosen on the test scores. We also tried to predict the total reading time from the XLM-R base embedding. The correlation for the embedding alone was 0.517 against 0.626 for word length and frequency. The embedding together with word length and frequency gave 0.622.
\item \textbf{LLM surprisal.} We tested the surprisal of the target word in its context from a language model as a third signal. With Qwen3 8B, it lowered the fusion by less than 0.001 and made Filipino worse. With the average of six language models, it lowered the fusion by 0.001.
\item \textbf{Fine-tuning XLM-R.} In an earlier setup without LOLO, we trained on the English CompLex data and tested on 2{,}827 MLSP 2024 instances. The best of 14 runs fine-tuning the whole encoder reached 0.461, against 0.551 with the encoder frozen. The best of six LoRA runs reached 0.462, against 0.515 with the encoder frozen.
\item \textbf{Few-shot prompt.} We used a prompt with six labelled examples from the trial split of the other languages. On the trial split, it was our best single prompt with 0.697. On the test split, it lowered the fusion by 0.012 as the only prompt and made five languages worse. As a fourth prompt next to our three prompts, it changed the fusion by less than 0.001.
\end{itemize}
