\section{Representation Geometry}
\label{app:tsne}
Figure~\ref{fig:tsne} projects the target vectors of the $7{,}211$ test instances with t-SNE
\citep{vandermaaten-hinton-2008-tsne}, before and after each language is centred on its own mean.
The three encoders do not share a space, so their vectors cannot be averaged coordinate by
coordinate; we concatenate them after scaling each to the same total variance, which makes a squared
distance in the concatenation the sum of the three encoders' squared distances, and distances are
all that t-SNE uses. In the concatenated space, excluding neighbours from the same sentence, $0.57$
of each vector's 15 nearest neighbours share its language before centring and $0.33$ after, against
$0.11$ by chance.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_tsne_encoders.png}
\caption{t-SNE of the target vectors, XLM-R base, LaBSE and multilingual E5 concatenated with equal
weight, on the eleven-language test split ($n=7{,}211$), before and after per-language centring
(PCA to 50 dimensions, perplexity 30, seed 0).}
\label{fig:tsne}
\end{figure*}

\section{Feature Attribution for Signal A}
\label{app:shap}
Figure~\ref{fig:shap} attributes signal~A, as it enters the fusion, to its inputs. Within a language,
signal~A is an affine function of six predictions, two arms for each of three encoders, and each
prediction is the mean of a ridge regression and a gradient-boosted tree. We compute Shapley values
for every model \citep{lundberg-lee-2017-unified}, in closed form for the ridge and with
interventional TreeSHAP for the tree \citep{lundberg-etal-2020-trees}, and combine them with the same
affine weights, so the attributions of each instance sum to its value of signal~A. The reference is
the instance's own language rather than the training data, because signal~A is standardised within
the language and a shift shared by a whole language changes nothing; the two indicator columns are
constant within a language, receive exactly zero, and are not shown. On average, word frequency moves
signal~A by $0.56$ of its within-language standard deviation and the embedding by $0.31$, while
subword count, capitalisation and syllable count stay below $0.06$. On Sinhala, which has no
frequency list, the embedding carries $0.81$ and subword count $0.15$.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{fig_shap_signal_a.pdf}
\caption{SHAP values of signal~A for the $7{,}211$ test instances, relative to the mean of each
instance's language. Colour is the feature value; the $2{,}304$ embedding dimensions of the three
encoders are summed into one row, in grey.}
\label{fig:shap}
\end{figure*}
